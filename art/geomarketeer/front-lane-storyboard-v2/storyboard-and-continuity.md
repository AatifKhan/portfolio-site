## Five beats

### 01 — The idea
Sid wanted a café of his own.
This busy café lane looked like a promising place.
Visual: tight front-lane view, with the vacant blue-awning unit between busy neighbors. No metro, offices, towers or detached central shop.

### 02 — The assumption
The cafés next door were busy.
Surely there was room for one more?
Visual: closer emphasis on that same empty unit and neighboring café activity. The site is in the row.

### 03 — The doubt
But what was bringing people here?
The metro explained some of the crowd. It didn’t tell Sid who would buy.
Visual: pull back and right, revealing shared parking, crossings and metro pedestrian routes. This is the first metro reveal.

### 04 — The context
He needed more than footfall.
Who lives and works nearby? What do they spend? What do the existing cafés already offer?
Visual: full neighborhood with the same front-row candidate, residences back left and offices upper right.
People, spending and competition are area-level questions/data categories, not measured results. Income cannot be inferred from the office buildings, or age and spending from individual figures. Final overlays require defined areas and verified data; sketch leader lines are schematic.

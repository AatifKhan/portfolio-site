# GeoMarketeer — five-click storyboard

Status: storyboard draft. Neighborhood composition approved and locked by the user on 2026-10-25.
Sid is an illustrative scenario. No measured neighborhood data or business outcomes are claimed.

## 01 — The opportunity
Headline: The cafés here were busy.
Support: Sid saw a promising place to open his own.
Visual: Frame the existing foreground cafés and their occupied terraces. Show the proposed unit across the street as a secondary anchor. Keep the metro, towers and offices outside the opening crop.
Purpose: Existing competition is Sid's reason for considering this location, not evidence that it is a bad site.

## 02 — The assumption
Headline: Surely there was room for one more?
Support: A busy café cluster made the location feel like a safe bet.
Visual: Connect the competing café row, crossing, proposed unit and adjacent parking. Keep the metro outside the frame. Do not move or open the candidate café.

## 03 — The reveal
Headline: Passing through isn’t the same as stopping.
Support: The metro explained some of the crowd. It didn’t tell Sid who would buy.
Visual: Widen towards the right to reveal the underground metro entrance and pedestrians following the sidewalk past the candidate. Same location and orientation.
Purpose: The reveal introduces uncertainty about demand; it does not prove commuters will never stop.

## 04 — The context
Headline: He needed more than footfall.
Support: Who lives and works nearby? What do they spend? What do the existing cafés already offer?
Visual: Pull back to the full approved composition, with residential towers back left, offices top right, metro beside the bustling street, candidate and parking at center, competitors below.
Area-level context categories:
- People: residents, workers, age groups.
- Spending: household income, spending patterns.
- Competition: existing cafés, offers.
These are questions and proposed data categories, not measured values. Do not infer income from a particular office building, age from individual figures, or customers from passing pedestrians. The storyboard's leader lines are schematic; actual data overlays must relate to a defined area and verified source.

## 05 — The response
Headline: Same corner. A clearer picture.
Support: GeoMarketeer helps businesses understand a neighborhood before choosing where to open.
Role: My role: UX Research & Interaction Design
Visual: Retain the full map and same candidate location, with quieter context labels. End the five primary story beats here. Optional exploration follows.
Purpose: Explain the product's value without inventing a best site, successful launch, failed café or performance metric.

## Pacing
Five primary narrative states. One advance action per state; the fifth closes the introduction and can lead into the case study or optional exploration. Final interaction semantics are for the later implementation.
